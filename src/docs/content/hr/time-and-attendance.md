Time off is requested by the employee, approved by a manager, and then appears automatically on the scheduling calendar.

That last part is the one that matters operationally.

## Requesting time off

From the employee portal:

1. Go to **Time and attendance → PTO and leave**.
2. Click **Request time off**.
3. Pick the type: vacation, sick, personal, unpaid.
4. Set the dates.
5. Add a note if it needs one.
6. Submit.

It goes to the manager for approval.

## Approving

Managers see requests in the manager portal.

Approve or decline promptly. A request sitting for two weeks is a person who cannot book a flight, and it is the most common complaint about any HR system.

Before approving, check the [scheduling calendar](/docs/jobs/scheduling), not just whether the person is free but whether the crew is.

## How it reaches scheduling

::: tip
Approved time off shows on the scheduling calendar as unavailable. This is the single most valuable connection in the HR portal. It is what stops a crew being scheduled around a vacation someone forgot about.
:::

It only works if requests are entered in the system. Time off agreed verbally and never entered is invisible to everyone doing the scheduling.

## Accrual policies

Accrual policies decide how time off is earned: how much, how often, whether it carries over, and any cap.

Policies typically vary by tenure and employment type. Set them up once; they run themselves.

Employees see their current balance when requesting, which removes most of the "how much do I have left?" traffic.

## Carryover and caps

Decide deliberately, and communicate it more than once.

::: warning
A use-it-or-lose-it policy nobody was reminded about produces a December where half the company is out at once. If you have a cap, remind people in October, not December.
:::

## Timesheets

Timesheets record hours worked. For field crews, hours logged against jobs also feed [job profitability](/docs/jobs/costs-and-profitability).

Encourage daily entry. Reconstructing a week on Friday afternoon produces round numbers that are wrong, and job costs built on them are wrong too.

## Unplanned absence

Sick days and emergencies get recorded after the fact rather than requested in advance. Record them anyway. A person's time-off record is incomplete without them, and balances drift.

## What to check monthly

1. Requests waiting on approval
2. Balances approaching a cap
3. Overlapping absences in the same crew
4. Timesheets not submitted
