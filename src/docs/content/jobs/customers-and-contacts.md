## Customers

A customer is a company you bill. Every job and every opportunity points at one.

A customer record holds the company name, address, category, assigned sales rep, and the full history of opportunities, jobs, and sites worked for them.

### Creating a customer

Go to **Customers** and click **New customer**. The name and address are the only things you truly need; everything else can be filled in later.

::: warning
Search before you create. Duplicate customers are the most common data problem in ampOS, and they are annoying to untangle: jobs, contacts, and history end up split across two records that look identical in a dropdown.
:::

### Customer categories

Categories group customers by type: industrial, healthcare, utility, commercial, whatever your company uses. They drive reporting and territory assignment. Administrators manage the list under **Customer categories**.

## Contacts

A contact is a person. Contacts belong to a customer.

Each contact has a name, title, email, phone, and an optional role on the job: the person who signs off, the person who grants site access, the person who gets the deliverable.

### Why contacts matter

Contacts are who receives things. When a deliverable goes out, it goes to a contact. When the customer portal is enabled, contacts are who log in. A customer with no contacts is a customer you cannot actually send anything to.

## How they attach to a job

A job points at one customer. Contacts from that customer become available on the job for site access, deliverable recipients, and the report header.

::: note
Sites are handled differently. A site belongs to the location, not to the customer, because the same building can be worked for different customers over time. See [Sites](/docs/jobs/sites).
:::

## Finding a customer's history

Open the customer record. You get:

- **Jobs.** Everything you have done for them.
- **Opportunities.** Everything you have quoted, won or lost.
- **Contacts.** Everyone you know there.
- **Sites.** The locations involved.

This page is the one to open before a renewal conversation or a walk-through.

## Merging duplicates

If two records for the same company exist, an administrator can consolidate them. Do not do it by hand by deleting one; that takes the job history with it. Ask an administrator.
