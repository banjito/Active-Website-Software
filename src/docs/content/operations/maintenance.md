Maintenance records keep the fleet running: what is due, what has been done, and what is late.

## Where it is

**Maintenance** under your division, or **Field Tech → Maintenance**.

## Scheduling maintenance

1. Open the maintenance schedule and click **Add**.
2. Pick the vehicle or equipment.
3. Choose the service type: oil change, inspection, calibration, annual service.
4. Set when it is due, by date or by mileage.
5. Save.

Recurring service should be scheduled as recurring, so the next one appears when this one is completed rather than depending on someone remembering.

## Recording completed service

Mark it complete, and record:

- What was done
- Who did it, in house or a shop
- Cost
- Mileage at service
- Anything found that was not the reason for the visit

::: tip
The last one is the valuable field. "Also noted rear tires at 4/32" is how a tire replacement becomes planned work instead of a roadside call.
:::

## Overdue maintenance

The schedule flags anything past due.

Work it weekly. Overdue maintenance is the most reliably ignored list in any company, and it stays ignored right up until a truck is down on the morning of a two-crew job.

## Taking something out of service

When a vehicle or piece of equipment should not be used, mark it out of service.

This matters for the same reason as everything else here: someone will otherwise assign it, load it, and drive two hours before discovering the problem.

Note why, and the expected return date if you have one.

## Maintenance and scheduling

A vehicle in the shop is a vehicle that cannot go to a job. Check the maintenance schedule when planning the week. Booking a crew around a truck that is due for service on Wednesday is avoidable.

See [Scheduling](/docs/jobs/scheduling).

## Instrument calibration

Calibration is maintenance, but it is managed through the lab because the dates feed directly into test reports. See [Equipment calibration](/docs/lab/equipment-calibration).

::: warning
Do not let an instrument's calibration lapse quietly. Unlike a late oil change, an expired calibration retroactively undermines every reading taken with it, and it is printed on the report that goes to the customer.
:::

## Costs

Maintenance costs are overhead, not job costs, so they do not attach to a specific job. But they are worth tracking, because a vehicle whose maintenance spend is climbing is telling you something about replacement timing.
