A customer's engineer reads three things in a report package: the executive summary, anything flagged as failing, and the comments. Everything else is evidence.

## Writing comments

A useful comment answers: what did you find, what does it mean, what should they do?

**Weak:**

- `OK`
- `See above`
- `Tested`

**Useful:**

- `Bushing 2 shows evidence of tracking on the porcelain. Photographed. Recommend replacement at next scheduled outage.`
- `Unit could not be tested. Customer would not de-energize feeder during production. Recommend testing at the spring outage.`
- `Insulation resistance has declined from 4.2 GΩ (2024) to 1.8 GΩ. Still above minimum, but trending down. Recommend annual rather than biennial testing.`

The third one is the kind of comment that gets your company called back next year.

## What to always comment on

- Anything that failed or is marginal
- Anything you could not test, and why
- Anything you fixed, adjusted, or cleaned while you were there
- Anything that surprised you
- Any recommendation

## Photos

Photos attach to the job's **Pictures** tab and can be pulled into reports and into the finished deliverable, so a photo is uploaded once and used wherever it is needed.

### What to photograph

| Shot | Why |
|---|---|
| **Nameplate** | Settles every later argument about ratings and serials |
| **As-found condition** | Proves what it looked like before you touched it |
| **Anything damaged** | The single most valuable photo in the package |
| **Instrument display during the test** | Backs up the reading if it is ever questioned |
| **The equipment with covers off** | Context for anyone reading the report later |
| **Anything you had to work around** | Blocked access, missing labels, wrong drawings |

Take more than you think you need. Storage is cheap; a return trip is not.

::: tip
Photograph nameplates before you start testing. Half the time the drawing is wrong, and finding that out at the end means re-entering the nameplate section on every report.
:::

### Photos in the deliverable

Photos included in a report appear in the finished package. Which means a blurry, badly-lit photo of a floor is going to a customer with your company's logo on the cover.

Take a second to frame the shot. Wipe the lens. It reads as professionalism, or the lack of it.

## Comments versus job notes

Two different things:

- **Report comments** are customer-facing. They go in the package.
- **Job notes** are internal. They never leave the company.

`Customer's maintenance guy was unhelpful and we lost two hours` is a job note. `Access to the equipment was delayed; testing completed the following day` is a report comment.

Know which one you are writing.
