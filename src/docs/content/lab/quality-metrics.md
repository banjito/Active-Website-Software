Quality metrics turn lab activity into numbers: how much came through, how long it took, and how much had to be done again.

## Where they are

**Lab → Quality metrics.**

## What to watch

| Metric | Tells you |
|---|---|
| **Throughput** | How much work went through in the period |
| **Turnaround time** | How long from received to returned |
| **Rework rate** | How much had to be redone |
| **Overdue calibrations** | How much of the register has lapsed |
| **On-time completion** | Whether commitments were met |

## Rework is the important one

Throughput measures activity. Rework measures whether the activity was any good.

A rising rework rate means something is wrong upstream: an unclear procedure, a new technician who needs support, an instrument that is drifting. It is the earliest signal you get, and it is the one worth investigating rather than just reporting.

::: tip
When rework rises, look at what changed six to eight weeks earlier. New people, new procedures, and new equipment show up in the rework rate on a delay.
:::

## Overdue calibrations

Track this as a percentage of the register, not a count. Five overdue instruments out of twenty is a crisis; five out of four hundred is a Tuesday.

The target is zero. See [Equipment calibration](/docs/lab/equipment-calibration).

## Reading the dashboard

Compare against the previous period, not against zero. The useful question is "is this getting better or worse", not "is this number big".

Watch for:

- Turnaround creeping up while throughput stays flat, which is a capacity problem forming
- Throughput up and rework up together, which means going too fast
- Both flat for months, which means the metrics are probably not being maintained

## Using metrics in a quality review

Bring them to the weekly meeting as scorecard numbers: on track or off track, no discussion. If one is off track, it becomes an issue and gets worked there.

See [Meetings](/docs/operations/meetings).

## What metrics will not tell you

Numbers show that something changed. They never show why.

A turnaround time that doubled could be a broken instrument, a technician on leave, a customer sending everything at once, or a procedure that got longer for good reasons. Go look before concluding.
