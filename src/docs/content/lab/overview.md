The lab portal manages the instruments your testing depends on.

If a meter's calibration lapses, every reading it took becomes questionable. The lab portal exists so that never comes as a surprise.

## What lives here

| Page | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Equipment calibration** | Every instrument, its calibration date, and what is coming due |
| **Testing procedures** | Written procedures the lab works to |
| **Certificates** | Generated calibration and test certificates |
| **Quality metrics** | Throughput, turnaround, and rework |

## Why field crews should care

The lab is not a separate world. Three things connect directly to field work:

1. **The test equipment section on every report** pulls from the calibration register. That is how a technician picks a calibrated instrument from a list instead of typing a serial from memory.
2. **An expired calibration date prints on the report** you send to a customer. It documents the problem in writing.
3. **Certificates** are what a customer's quality department asks for when they audit you.

See [Equipment tables](/docs/reports/equipment-tables).

## The one rule

::: danger
If an instrument's calibration was expired on the date a test was performed, do not put that reading in a report you intend to send. Re-test with a calibrated instrument.
:::

There is no way to fix this afterwards. The date is on the record.

## Who works here

Lab technicians manage calibration, procedures, and certificates. Everyone else mostly consumes what the lab produces: a list of calibrated instruments that is accurate.
