The job page is where most of the work happens. It has a header with the essentials and a row of tabs beneath it.

## The header

Always visible, no matter which tab you are on:

- Job number and title
- Customer and site
- Status, which you can click to change if your role allows
- Due date
- Quoted amount

## Overview

The default tab. Scope, dates, assigned people, and a summary of what is on the job. Start here when you open a job you have not seen before.

## Assets

The equipment list, and the tab field techs live in.

Each row is one piece of equipment with the report attached to it. The columns show the asset name, its identifier, the report type, and the report's status.

You can:

- **Add assets** one at a time, or **bulk import** a list from a spreadsheet.
- **Link sub-assets to a parent**, so a switchgear lineup and its individual breakers group together instead of scattering through the list.
- **Filter by status** to see just what is `In progress`, `Ready for review`, or `Approved`.
- **Sort by several columns at once.** Hold Shift and click a second header.

Full detail in [Assets](/docs/jobs/assets).

## Reports

A flat list of every report on the job, regardless of which asset it belongs to. Useful when you want to see review progress across the whole job at a glance rather than hunting asset by asset.

## Deliverables

The packages built for this customer. Create one, add approved reports to it, attach a cover letter and executive summary, and generate the combined PDF.

See [Deliverables overview](/docs/deliverables/overview).

## Tracking

Schedule, resource allocation, SLA status, and submittals. This is the project manager's tab: who is going, when, and whether the promised dates still hold.

## Profitability

Quoted amount versus actual cost, and the margin between them. Labor, materials, expenses, and change orders all roll up here.

See [Costs and profitability](/docs/jobs/costs-and-profitability).

## T&M expenses

Time and materials logged against the job: hours, mileage, per diem, purchased materials. These flow into profitability and, for T&M jobs, into the invoice.

## Notes

Internal running commentary. Notes are not customer-facing. Use them for the things that do not fit anywhere else: which gate the crew should use, why the third breaker got skipped, who to call about access.

## Pictures

Site and equipment photos. Photos uploaded here can be pulled into reports and deliverables rather than re-uploaded.

## After action

The post-job debrief. What went wrong, what to do differently, what to tell the customer next year.

## Report audit

A check across every report on the job: missing required fields, unfilled test values, reports still sitting in `In progress`. Run this before you build a deliverable and you will not discover a half-empty report at the last minute.

::: tip
The report audit tab is the single most useful thing on this page for anyone assembling deliverables. Run it first, fix what it finds, then build the package.
:::

## Equipment assets

Company test equipment used on this job, and which calibration dates applied. This is what lets you answer "what was the calibration status of the instrument that took this reading?" when a customer asks.
