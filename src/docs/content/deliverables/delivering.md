The package is approved. Now it has to reach a human at the customer, and your system has to know that it did.

## Sending it

Download the generated PDF and send it to the customer contact, or make it available through the customer portal if your company has that enabled. See [Customer portal](/docs/integrations/customer-portal).

Send it to a **contact**, not to a generic company address. A package sent to `info@` is a package nobody reads. See [Customers and contacts](/docs/jobs/customers-and-contacts).

## Marking it delivered

Set the deliverable's status to **Delivered**.

This is not bookkeeping for its own sake. It is what tells everyone else the job is ready to move on, and it is the evidence you sent it when someone asks in four months.

## Then mark the job ready to bill

Once the package is out, move the job to **Ready to bill**. That sends the handoff email to accounting and puts the job on the next morning's ready-to-bill report.

Run the billing checklist first. See [Billing a job](/docs/jobs/billing).

::: tip
Deliverable delivered → job ready to bill. Doing these together is the habit that keeps finished work from sitting unbilled for weeks.
:::

## What to say in the email

Short. The PDF carries the detail.

- What is attached
- The one-sentence headline: all satisfactory, or findings requiring attention
- Who to call with questions

If there is an urgent finding, telephone them as well. Do not rely on an attachment being opened.

## If the customer asks for changes

Depends on what kind of change.

**A genuine error on your side.** Issue a corrected package, clearly marked as a revision, with a note saying what changed.

**They want the same data presented differently.** That is new work. It may be a change order. See [Change orders](/docs/jobs/change-orders).

**They disagree with a finding.** Do not just remove it. Discuss it, and if you were wrong, issue a correction that says so.

::: danger
Never edit a delivered package silently. The customer holds a version with a date on it. If your copy quietly changes, the two no longer match, and when it matters (a warranty claim, an incident, a dispute) neither of you can prove what was actually sent.
:::

## Keeping the record

Delivered packages stay attached to the job permanently. Closing or billing the job does not remove them.

That means next year's crew opens last year's job and sees exactly what the customer was told, which is where good repeat maintenance work starts.
