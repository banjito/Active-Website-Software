Performance management runs on **cycles**: a review period opens, managers and employees complete their parts, and the results land in the employee record.

## Review cycles

A cycle defines the period, who is included, and the deadline.

### Running one

1. Go to **HR → Performance → Review cycles**.
2. Create a cycle. Name it for the period, `H1 2026`.
3. Set the review period and the completion deadline.
4. Include the relevant employees.
5. Open it.

Managers and employees are notified. Both complete their parts, then the review is discussed and finalized.

::: tip
Open the cycle at least three weeks before the deadline. A review written the night before the deadline is a review nobody got value from.
:::

## Employee goals

Goals set what someone is working toward between reviews.

Good ones are specific and checkable: `Complete NETA Level 3 certification by Q3`, not `improve technically`.

Set them at the review and check them mid-period. A goal reviewed once a year is a goal nobody thought about for eleven months.

## Feedback tools

Feedback recorded through the year, rather than only at review time.

This is what stops a review being a recollection of the last six weeks. A note written the week something happened is worth ten written from memory in December.

::: tip
Managers: log one note per direct report per month. It takes two minutes and it changes review quality more than any other single habit.
:::

## What a review should cover

1. **Goals from last cycle.** Met, partly met, not met, and why.
2. **How the work was done**, not just what.
3. **Growth.** What they want next, what they need to get there.
4. **Goals for next cycle.**
5. **Anything the manager should be doing differently.**

Point 5 is the one people skip and the one that tells you whether the conversation was honest.

## What a review is not

- **A surprise.** Anything in a review should have been said when it happened.
- **A compensation negotiation.** Related, but keep them as separate conversations or the review becomes a haggle.
- **A form to complete.** The record is not the point; the conversation is.

## Where results go

Completed reviews attach to the [employee profile](/docs/hr/employee-profiles), alongside title and compensation history.

That combination is what makes a promotion or pay decision defensible a year later.

## Performance goals versus sales goals

Different things.

- **Performance goals** are about a person's development, set in a review cycle.
- **[Sales goals](/docs/sales/goals)** are revenue and activity targets in the sales portal.

A sales rep has both, and confusing them makes the review about the number instead of the person.
