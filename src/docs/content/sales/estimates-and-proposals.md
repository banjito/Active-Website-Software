An estimate turns a scope into a number. A proposal is the document you send.

## Building an estimate

1. Go to **Sales → Estimates**, or start one from an opportunity.
2. Open the estimate sheet.
3. Apply a **preset** if one fits. It fills in your standard rates and line items so you are editing rather than starting blank. See [Estimating presets](/docs/sales/estimating-presets).
4. Add **line items**: labor, equipment, materials, travel, per diem.
5. Set **quantities**. On a testing job this is usually a count of devices: 12 breakers, 3 transformers, 400 feet of cable.
6. Review the total.

## Scope quantities

Testing work prices by count, so the estimate is built around quantities of equipment rather than lump-sum guesses.

Getting these right is most of estimating accuracy. Count off the one-line drawing, not off memory, and write down what you counted. When the customer says "you only quoted twelve breakers and there are nineteen," you want the count in the estimate rather than in your head.

::: tip
When the equipment list is uncertain, quote what you can count and note the assumption in the proposal. That turns a future argument into a change order. See [Change orders](/docs/jobs/change-orders).
:::

## Generating the proposal

From the estimate, generate the proposal document. It comes out branded with your company's logo, address, and terms. Nobody pastes a logo into anything.

Two formats are typically used:

- **Full proposal.** Scope, pricing, terms, exclusions. For anything substantial.
- **Letter proposal.** A shorter letter format. For small or repeat work where a twelve-page document is overkill.

## What to check before sending

1. Customer name and address
2. The scope description. Is it what you actually discussed?
3. Quantities
4. The total
5. **Exclusions.** The most valuable paragraph in the document. What is not included: de-energization, lifts, after-hours work, repairs found during testing.
6. Validity period

::: warning
The exclusions paragraph is what stops "well, we assumed that was included." Write it every time, and be specific.
:::

## Sending it

Send it to a named contact, not a generic company address. Then move the opportunity to **Quote**. See [Opportunities](/docs/sales/opportunities).

## Revising

Customers ask for changes. Revise the estimate and generate a new proposal rather than editing the PDF.

Keep the old version. When they ask why the price moved, you want both documents.

## Copying an estimate to another opportunity

Repeat work barely changes year to year. Copy last year's estimate onto this year's opportunity, adjust the rates and quantities, and you are most of the way done.

## When you win

Move the opportunity to **Awarded** and create the job from it. The quoted amount carries across into the job, which is what makes profitability meaningful.
