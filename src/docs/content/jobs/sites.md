A site is a physical place: a plant, a hospital campus, a substation, a data center.

## Why sites are separate from customers

This is the part that surprises people.

A site belongs to the **location**, not permanently to one customer. The same building can be worked for different customers over time. You test the switchgear for the property management company one year and for the incoming tenant the next.

If sites belonged to customers, that history would split in two and the equipment record would start over. Instead:

- The **site** carries the equipment, the history, and the location details.
- The **customer** lives on the job.

So when you look at a site, you see every job ever done there, no matter who paid for it.

## What a site holds

- Name and address
- Directions, gate codes, parking notes, badge requirements
- Site contacts: the plant electrician, the security desk
- The assets that live there
- Every job worked at that location

## Creating a site

Go to **Sites** and click **New site**. Or create one directly from a job when you set the job's location.

Give it a name a technician can recognize from a truck: `Decatur Plant, Building 4`, not `Site 12`.

## Site assets

Assets attach to sites, not just to jobs. That is what makes year-over-year testing work: next year's PM job pulls the same transformer record, and you can compare this year's readings against last year's without retyping the nameplate.

::: tip
Spend the extra five minutes naming assets consistently the first time you work a site. `T-1`, `T-2`, `T-3` beats `Transformer 1`, `XFMR 2`, `main xfmr`. Next year's crew will thank you, and trending only works if the names match.
:::

## Access notes

The most valuable field on a site record is the one nobody fills in: how to actually get in.

Write down the gate, the guard-shack phone number, whether you need a badge, which door is unlocked before 7am, and who to call when the escort does not show. Put it in the site notes, not the job notes. Job notes disappear when the job closes; site notes are there next time.

## Finding work at a site

Open the site and you get its full job history, its assets, and its contacts. This is the fastest way to answer "have we been here before, and what did we find?"
