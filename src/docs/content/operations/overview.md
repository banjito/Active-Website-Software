Operations is what it takes to keep crews supplied and moving: buying things, tracking vehicles and field equipment, managing shared resources, and running the meetings that keep everyone pointed the same direction.

## What lives here

| Area | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Vendors and POs** | Who you buy from, and what you bought for which job |
| **Resources** | Shared equipment and people, and who has them booked |
| **Field equipment** | Test gear and the vehicles it lives in |
| **Maintenance** | Service due, service done, service overdue |
| **Meetings** | The weekly rhythm |

## Why it connects to job profitability

Every purchase order should point at a job. That is what turns "we spent $4,200 with that vendor last month" into "this job cost $4,200 in materials."

::: warning
A PO with no job attached is a cost that disappears. It comes out of the bank account and never appears in any job's margin, which makes every job look more profitable than it was.
:::

See [Costs and profitability](/docs/jobs/costs-and-profitability).

## The weekly rhythm

Most companies running ampOS settle into something like:

- **Monday morning.** The weekly PO and jobs status reports arrive by email. Review what is open.
- **Monday.** Weekly meeting. Work the issues list.
- **Daily 8:00 AM.** The ready-to-bill report. Clear it.
- **Daily 12:00 PM.** The review notification. Reviewers clear the queue in the afternoon.

Those four things, done consistently, are most of what keeps work from sitting.

## Who works here

Office admins mostly, plus whoever manages the fleet. Field crews touch equipment and maintenance; project managers touch resources.
