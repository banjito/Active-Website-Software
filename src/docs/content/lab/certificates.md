Certificates are generated documents produced from lab records, carrying your company branding and the underlying data.

## Generating one

1. Go to **Lab → Certificates**.
2. Click **New certificate**.
3. Pick the instrument or the test the certificate covers.
4. Confirm the data pulled through.
5. Generate.

Because it is generated from records rather than typed, the certificate and the register cannot disagree.

## What goes on one

- Your company details and branding
- What the certificate covers: instrument, serial, or the test performed
- Date performed
- Reference standards used
- Results and tolerances
- Traceability: which reference standards, and their own calibration
- Who performed it, and who approved it
- Certificate number and issue date

::: note
Traceability is the part customers' quality departments actually check. A certificate that does not say what it was measured against is not worth much to an auditor.
:::

## Sending one to a customer

Download and send it, or include it in a deliverable when it belongs with a job's package.

Calibration certificates for the instruments used on a job are a common customer request, and some contracts require them with every report package. Worth asking at the start of a contract rather than scrambling at the end.

## Reissuing

If a certificate has an error, issue a **corrected** one that says it supersedes the original. Do not quietly replace it.

::: danger
Never edit an issued certificate. The customer holds a copy with a number and a date on it. If your copy silently changes, neither of you can prove what was issued, which defeats the entire purpose of a certificate.
:::

## Numbering

Certificate numbers should be sequential and never reused. A reused number makes two different documents indistinguishable in a customer's records.

## Keeping them

Certificates stay attached to their records permanently. When a customer asks for the calibration certificate for the meter that took a reading three years ago, that chain is: report → test equipment section → instrument → certificate.

That chain only holds if the test equipment section on the report was filled in. See [Equipment tables](/docs/reports/equipment-tables).
