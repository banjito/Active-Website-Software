A goal puts a number on what someone is expected to produce, and then tracks actual results against it.

## Creating a goal

1. Go to **Sales → Goals** and click **New goal**.
2. Give it a **title**, such as `Q3 Revenue, North Alabama`.
3. Pick the **type** (below).
4. Pick the **scope**: Individual, Team, Department, or Company.
5. Set the **target value**.
6. Set the **period**: Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly, or Custom.
7. Set the start and end dates.
8. Assign an **owner**.
9. Save.

## Goal types

| Type | Measures | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | Dollars won | The main number for most reps |
| **Deals** | Count of opportunities won | Balancing a rep who only chases whales |
| **Units** | Count of anything you define | Volume work |
| **Meetings** | Customer meetings held | New reps, or rebuilding a quiet territory |
| **Calls** | Calls made | Early-stage activity |

::: tip
Revenue goals measure the outcome. Meetings and calls measure the behavior that produces it. A rep who is behind on revenue and also behind on meetings has a diagnosable problem; one who is behind on revenue while ahead on meetings has a different one entirely.
:::

## Scope

| Scope | Applies to |
|---|---|
| **Individual** | One person |
| **Team** | A group of reps |
| **Department** | Sales as a whole |
| **Company** | Everyone |

Individual and company goals can coexist and should. The company number is the one that matters; individual numbers are how it gets hit.

## The goals dashboard

**Sales → Goals → Dashboard** shows progress against every goal: where each stands, how much of the period has elapsed, and whether the pace is enough.

Pace matters more than position. A rep at 40% of a quarterly goal is fine in week five and in trouble in week eleven.

## Managing goals across a team

**Goals → Management** is the bulk view for setting and adjusting many goals at once, useful at the start of a period rather than clicking through one at a time.

## Editing a goal mid-period

You can. Be careful about why.

Adjusting a target because the business genuinely changed, like a major customer being acquired or a division closing, is legitimate. Adjusting because the number is not going to be hit defeats the purpose of having set it.

::: warning
A goal quietly lowered in month three is worse than a goal missed. Missing tells you something; moving the line tells you nothing and teaches everyone that the targets are decorative.
:::

## Goals and territories

Territories carry revenue targets too, and they are not the same as individual goals. A territory target is about coverage of an area. An individual goal is about a person's production. A rep covering two territories has one goal and two territory numbers.

See [Territories](/docs/sales/territories).
