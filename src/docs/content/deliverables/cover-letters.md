The cover letter does two jobs in ampOS. It is the professional introduction to the package, and it is where you choose which reports go into it.

## What it says

A cover letter is short. Three or four paragraphs:

1. **What this is.** "Enclosed are the results of acceptance testing performed at your Decatur facility on 12-14 March 2026."
2. **What was tested.** A sentence or a short list. Not a full inventory; the reports are the inventory.
3. **Headline findings.** One sentence. "All tested equipment was found satisfactory," or "Two findings require attention; see the executive summary."
4. **Who to contact.** A name and a number.

## What it should not say

- A recap of every reading. That is what the reports are for.
- Hedging language that leaves the customer unsure whether anything is wrong.
- Boilerplate nobody reads.

::: tip
If a customer's engineer reads only the cover letter and the executive summary, they should still know whether they have a problem. Write for that reader.
:::

## Selecting reports

Report selection lives on the cover letter, which is why you create the cover letter before generating the package.

Choose the approved reports belonging to this deliverable. On a job producing several packages, select only the slice for this one.

Unapproved reports are not available. That is the gate. See [Review and approval](/docs/reports/review-and-approval).

## Reusing a cover letter

Cover letters follow a house style, so most of the text stays the same job to job. Copy your last one and change the specifics: dates, site, scope, findings.

What must change every time:

- Customer name and address
- Site and dates
- Scope description
- The findings sentence

::: danger
The classic failure is sending a customer a cover letter with the previous customer's name still in it. Read the letter before generating. Every time.
:::

## Locking

When a deliverable is approved or sent, its cover letter **locks**, and ampOS records who locked it and when.

A locked letter cannot be edited. The customer holds a specific version of that document, and letting your copy quietly drift from theirs helps nobody.

If a delivered package needs correcting, issue a corrected package that says what changed. Do not attempt to rewrite history.

## Signatures

Cover letters carry signature blocks drawn from signature profiles, so you are not retyping names, titles, and phone numbers on every package. See [Signature profiles](/docs/deliverables/signature-profiles).

## Branding

Cover letters use your company's logo, colors, and address automatically. Nobody needs to paste a logo into anything.
