Field equipment tracking answers three questions: what do we own, where is it, and is it fit to use?

## The equipment register

**Field equipment** under your division, or **Field Tech → Field equipment**.

Each item carries:

- What it is: make, model, description
- Serial number
- Asset tag
- Current assignment: which vehicle or person
- Condition
- Calibration status, for instruments
- Service history

## Adding equipment

1. Open the equipment list and click **Add**.
2. Enter make, model, and serial.
3. Give it an asset tag if you use them.
4. Set its category, which decides which reports offer it in the test equipment section.
5. Assign it to a vehicle or a person.
6. Save.

::: note
Category matters more than it looks. It is what makes a transformer report offer megohmmeters rather than every instrument the company owns. Getting it wrong means technicians type instruments in by hand instead of picking them.
:::

## Assigning equipment

Assign to a **vehicle** for gear that lives on a truck, or to a **person** for anything individually issued.

Reassign when it moves. An accurate register is worth having; a stale one is worse than none, because people trust it once and then stop.

## Vehicles

Vehicles are tracked alongside the gear:

- Identification and registration
- Assigned driver or crew
- Mileage
- Service history and what is due

## Calibration

Instruments carry calibration dates, managed through the lab. Those dates feed the test equipment section on reports, so a technician picks a calibrated instrument from a list.

::: warning
Equipment past its calibration date should be taken out of service, not quietly used. A reading from an out-of-calibration instrument is not a reading you can defend, and the expired date is printed on the report you send.
:::

See [Equipment calibration](/docs/lab/equipment-calibration).

## Reporting a problem

When something is damaged, faulty, or behaving oddly, record it on the equipment rather than mentioning it to someone.

Include what happened, when, and whether it is still usable. A meter that reads high intermittently is much more dangerous than one that is obviously broken, because it keeps being used.

## Equipment on a job

The job's **Equipment assets** tab records which instruments went out on which job.

That is what lets you answer, months later, "what was the calibration status of the instrument that took this reading?" It is a question that only ever gets asked when the answer matters.

## Before a job

1. Confirm the gear you need is assigned to your vehicle and actually in it.
2. Check calibration dates cover the job dates, not just today.
3. Report anything damaged before you leave, not from site.
