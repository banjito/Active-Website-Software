A deliverable is what the customer gets. It bundles approved reports with a cover letter and an executive summary into a single PDF with consistent page numbering and your company's branding.

Reports are your evidence. The deliverable is your product.

## What is in one

| Part | Required | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Cover letter** | Yes | The professional introduction, and where reports are selected |
| **Executive summary** | Optional | Findings and recommendations in plain language |
| **Reports** | Yes | Every approved report you selected, in order |

All of it comes out as one combined PDF, ready to send.

## The statuses

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | Being assembled. Fully editable. |
| **In review** | Submitted for approval. |
| **Approved** | Signed off, ready to send. |
| **Rejected** | Sent back, with a reason attached. |
| **Delivered** | The customer has it. |

## The flow

1. Reports are written and **approved**. Unapproved reports cannot go in a package.
2. Someone creates a deliverable on the job and writes the cover letter.
3. Reports are selected into it.
4. An executive summary is added, if the job warrants one.
5. The combined PDF is generated.
6. It goes to review.
7. Approved, then delivered.
8. The job moves to **ready to bill**.

## Document locking

When a deliverable is approved or sent, its cover letter **locks**. ampOS records who locked it and when.

This is deliberate. The customer has a specific version of that document; letting it be quietly edited afterwards means your copy and their copy stop matching, and neither of you can tell.

::: warning
If a delivered package turns out to be wrong, the fix is a corrected package with a note explaining what changed, not a silent edit of the original.
:::

## Several deliverables on one job

Common and encouraged. A large job might produce one package per building, one per phase, or one per outage window.

Each deliverable selects its own reports, so a report can appear in the package it belongs to without dragging in everything else on the job.

## Before you start

Run the job's **report audit** tab. It lists every report that is unfinished, unapproved, or missing required fields across the whole job.

Doing this first is the difference between assembling a deliverable once and assembling it three times.
