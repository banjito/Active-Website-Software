An employee profile is the master record for a person.

## What it holds

| Section | Contains |
|---|---|
| **Details** | Name, contact, emergency contact, start date, division |
| **Job title history** | Every role they have held, with dates |
| **Compensation history** | Every pay change, with dates and reasons |
| **Documents** | Signed forms, certifications, agreements, with version tracking |
| **Org chart position** | Who they report to, who reports to them |
| **Custom tabs** | Fields your company added |

## Creating a profile

Most profiles are created by onboarding rather than by hand. When an offer is accepted and onboarding starts, the profile is created as part of that flow.

Create one manually only for people who joined before ampOS, or for cases onboarding did not cover.

## Job title history

Record title changes rather than overwriting the current title.

::: warning
Overwriting the title destroys the history. Six months later, "when did they move into the lead role?" has no answer, and that question comes up in reviews, compensation discussions, and disputes.
:::

## Compensation history

Every pay change gets a record: the new amount, the effective date, and the reason (merit, promotion, market adjustment, correction).

The reason field is the one that matters. A list of numbers tells you what someone earns. A list of numbers with reasons tells you whether your pay decisions have been consistent, which is the question that eventually gets asked.

Compensation is the most tightly restricted data in the portal.

## Documents

Signed agreements, certifications, licenses, training records.

**Version tracking** keeps superseded versions rather than replacing them. When a policy is reissued and re-signed, both the old signed copy and the new one remain, which is the point of having signatures at all.

::: tip
Certifications with expiry dates, such as NETA certifications, licenses, and medical cards, are worth reviewing quarterly. An expired certification on a technician who is out testing is a problem you want to find internally.
:::

## The org chart

Shows reporting relationships. Keep it current: it drives who approves whose time off and who completes whose review.

An out-of-date org chart means time-off requests route to someone who left.

## The call list

An internal directory of names and phone numbers, drawn from profiles. Useful in the field, which is exactly where an out-of-date phone number costs you.

## Custom tabs

Companies track things the standard fields do not cover: union membership, specific certifications, equipment issued. Custom tabs add those fields without changing anything else.
