An executive summary is optional in ampOS and essential in practice on any job with findings.

The audience is not an electrical engineer. It is a plant manager or a facilities director deciding whether to spend money. Write for them.

## What goes in it

### Scope

Two sentences. What was tested, where, and when.

### Findings

The things that are wrong, ordered by how much they matter. For each one:

- **What.** `Transformer T-2, bushing 2, evidence of tracking on the porcelain.`
- **Why it matters.** `Tracking can progress to flashover, which would take the transformer and the load it serves out of service.`
- **What to do.** `Replace the bushing at the next scheduled outage.`
- **How urgent.** `Before next summer's peak load.`

### Satisfactory results

Say so plainly. "All other tested equipment was found within acceptable limits." A customer who paid for testing deserves to be told the good news too.

### Recommendations

What should happen next, and when. Include the next test interval.

## How to write it

Plain words. Short sentences. No jargon without explanation.

**Instead of:** `IR readings on T-2 winding 1 were 1.8 GΩ corrected, below the trend established in prior years, indicating possible moisture ingress.`

**Write:** `Insulation resistance on Transformer T-2 has dropped noticeably compared to previous years. This usually means moisture is getting into the windings. It is still within acceptable limits, but the trend is going the wrong way. We recommend testing this unit annually instead of every two years, and checking the enclosure seals.`

The second version is longer and it is the one that gets acted on.

## Ranking findings

If everything is urgent, nothing is. Use three levels and mean them:

| Level | Means |
|---|---|
| **Immediate** | A safety or outage risk now. Call, do not just write. |
| **Near term** | Address at the next planned outage. |
| **Monitor** | Not a problem yet. Watch the trend. |

::: warning
If you found something genuinely dangerous, telephone the customer the day you find it. The executive summary documents it; it is not how you communicate it.
:::

## Signatures

Executive summaries carry signature blocks: project manager, reviewed by, work performed by. These come from signature profiles rather than being retyped each time. See [Signature profiles](/docs/deliverables/signature-profiles).

## When to skip it

A routine PM with no findings on a handful of panels does not need one. A cover letter saying "all tested equipment was found satisfactory" covers it.

Anything with a finding, anything the customer is spending real money on, and anything going to a new customer gets one.

## Length

One page. Two if the job was large and there are several findings.

If it runs to four pages, it has stopped being an executive summary and become another report.
