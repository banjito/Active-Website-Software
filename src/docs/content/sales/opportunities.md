An opportunity is a piece of work you are trying to win.

## Creating one

1. Go to **Sales → Opportunities**.
2. Click **New opportunity**.
3. Pick the **customer**. Create the customer first if they do not exist. See [Customers and contacts](/docs/jobs/customers-and-contacts).
4. Give it a **title** that says what the work is. `Annual switchgear PM, Building 4`, not `PM work`.
5. Set the **expected value**. Your best estimate now; refine it when the estimate is built.
6. Set the **proposal due date**, meaning when the customer expects your quote, not when you expect to win.
7. Set the **division** that would do the work.
8. Save.

The opportunity gets a **quote number** automatically.

## The pipeline stages

| Stage | Means |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | They know you exist. Early conversation. |
| **Interest** | They have a need and are engaging about it. |
| **Quote** | You have sent a proposal. |
| **Decision** | They are deciding. |
| **Decision - Forecasted Win** | Deciding, and you expect to win. |
| **Decision - Forecast Lose** | Deciding, and you expect to lose. |
| **Awarded** | Won. |
| **Lost** | Lost. |
| **No Quote** | You declined to bid. |

### Using the two forecast stages honestly

`Decision - Forecasted Win` and `Decision - Forecast Lose` exist so the forecast reflects what you actually believe, not what you hope.

::: warning
Marking everything as a forecasted win produces a pipeline number nobody can plan against. If you think you are losing it, say so. The forecast is more useful when it is right than when it is optimistic.
:::

## Probability

Set the probability alongside the stage. Stage is where the deal is; probability is how likely it is. A deal can sit in `Decision` at 20% or at 80%, and those are very different things for forecasting.

## Notes and interactions

Log every meaningful conversation against the opportunity: what they said, what you promised, what you owe them.

This is the difference between picking up a stalled deal in March and starting it over.

## Marking one lost

Record it, and record **why**. Price, timing, incumbent, scope, no decision.

Lost reasons are the most useful sales data your company has. Six months of "lost on price" is a pricing conversation. Six months of "lost on timing" is a capacity conversation.

Do not leave dead deals sitting in `Decision` because marking them lost feels bad. A pipeline full of ghosts is worse than a smaller honest one.

## Turning a win into a job

Move the opportunity to **Awarded**, then create a job from it.

The customer, title, scope, and quoted amount carry across. That last one matters: a job created from an awarded opportunity has a quoted amount, so [profitability](/docs/jobs/costs-and-profitability) works from day one. A job created from scratch usually does not.

::: tip
Always create the job from the opportunity rather than starting a fresh one. It takes the same amount of time and you get the money linkage for free.
:::

## Sorting and filtering

The opportunity list sorts by quote number, created date, or proposal due date. Filter by stage, division, rep, and customer.

The most useful view for a rep is: proposal due date ascending, filtered to your own opportunities, stages `Interest` and `Quote`. That is your week.

## The calendar view

Opportunities also appear on a calendar by expected close date. See [Pipeline calendar](/docs/sales/pipeline-calendar).
