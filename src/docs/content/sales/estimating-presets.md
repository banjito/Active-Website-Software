A preset is a saved set of rates and standard line items. Presets stop every estimator from inventing prices, which is how two quotes for the same work end up 40% apart.

## What a preset holds

- **Labor rates.** By role, and by shift where they differ. Straight time, overtime, double time.
- **Standard line items.** The tests you sell repeatedly, priced per device.
- **Equipment and travel rates.** Trucks, test sets, mileage, per diem.

## Creating one

1. Go to **Sales → Estimating presets**.
2. Click **New preset**.
3. Name it for how it will be chosen: `Standard Industrial 2026`, `Utility Contract Rates`, `Emergency Callout`.
4. Add the rates and line items.
5. Save.

## Using one

Apply the preset when you start an estimate. It fills in the rates and standard items, and you edit from there rather than starting blank.

::: tip
A preset is a starting point, not a price list you must obey. Adjust for the job in front of you: access difficulty, night work, a customer who needs three site visits instead of one.
:::

## How many to have

Enough to cover genuinely different pricing situations, few enough that picking is obvious. Most companies need three to five:

- Standard commercial and industrial
- A negotiated contract customer or two, at their agreed rates
- Emergency and after-hours
- Whatever your specialty division charges

Twenty presets is a signal that you are pricing ad hoc and recording it afterwards.

## Keeping them current

::: warning
An out-of-date preset is worse than none. It puts a wrong number on a proposal with the appearance of authority, and nobody double-checks a rate that came from the system.
:::

Review presets when labor costs change, at least annually. Put the year in the name so an old one is obvious at a glance.

## Retiring one

When rates change, create a new preset rather than editing the old one in place. Estimates already sent kept their numbers, and you want to be able to see what the rates were when a quote went out.

## Presets and margin

A preset carries your intended margin inside its rates. Which means:

- If jobs priced off a preset consistently come in under margin, the preset is wrong, not the crew.
- [Job profitability](/docs/jobs/costs-and-profitability) is the feedback loop. Check actual margin against the preset periodically, and adjust the rates rather than blaming execution.
