An offer letter is generated from the candidate and the approved requisition, routed for internal approval, then sent to the candidate to sign electronically.

## Generating an offer letter

1. Go to **HR → Offers → Offer letters**.
2. Start a new offer, or generate one from a candidate.
3. Confirm the **compensation and position details**: title, pay, start date, reporting line, employment type.
4. Generate the letter.

It comes out with your company branding and standard language.

## Compensation and position details

Fill these in carefully. They flow into the letter, and after acceptance they become the starting point of the employee's [compensation history](/docs/hr/employee-profiles).

Check the offered amount against the requisition's approved range. A mismatch between what was approved and what was offered is the kind of thing found much later, in an awkward conversation.

## Offer approvals

Offers route for internal approval before going out.

::: warning
Never send an offer verbally before it has been approved. Walking back a number you already said out loud damages the relationship before the person has started, and it happens more often than anyone admits.
:::

## Sending for e-signature

Once approved, send it to the candidate. They receive a link, review the letter, and sign electronically. No printing, no scanning, no photograph of a signature on a kitchen table.

## Tracking signature status

The offer shows where it is: sent, viewed, signed, declined.

`Viewed` but not signed for several days is a signal, not a delay. Call them. It usually means they have a question they are not asking, or a competing offer.

## When it is signed

The signed copy is stored against the record automatically, and the record becomes the basis for onboarding.

Then start [onboarding](/docs/hr/onboarding). The gap between signature and first day is where good hires get lost. A candidate who signs and then hears nothing for three weeks starts wondering.

## When it is declined

Record the reason. Declined-offer reasons are as useful as lost-deal reasons in sales: a pattern of declines on compensation is a market problem, and a pattern on start date is a process problem.

## E-signature and recordkeeping

Signed documents are retained with an audit trail: who signed, when, from where. That record is what makes an electronic signature defensible.

See [Compliance](/docs/hr/compliance).
