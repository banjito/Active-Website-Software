The pipeline calendar shows opportunities on the dates they are expected to close. It is the fastest way to see whether next quarter is full or empty.

## Opening it

**Sales → Pipeline calendar**, or the calendar view on the opportunities list.

## Reading it

Each opportunity appears on its expected close date, with its value.

What to look for:

- **A thin month.** Work that has to be sold now, not then. Lead time means an empty March is a February problem.
- **A pile-up on one date.** Usually means people are entering the end of the month rather than a real date.
- **Deals in the past.** An expected close date that has gone by without a decision is a deal that needs a phone call.

::: warning
Deals sitting behind today's date are the most common problem this view exposes. They quietly inflate the pipeline number while representing nothing. Work them or mark them lost.
:::

## Filtering

Filter by rep, division, territory, and stage.

Two useful views:

- **By rep.** What one person's next ninety days look like.
- **Stage `Decision` only.** Everything actually being decided right now.

## Changing an expected close

Drag the opportunity to a new date, or edit it directly.

Move dates when reality moves. A date pushed three times is telling you something about the deal, and the pattern is only visible if you record it honestly rather than nudging it a week at a time.

## Using it in a pipeline review

Open it, filter to the current quarter, and go date by date. For each deal: what has to happen next, who owns it, by when.

Deals with no next step are not deals. They are hopes with a dollar value attached.

## Proposal due dates

Expected close and proposal due are different dates. The calendar shows expected close; the opportunity list sorts by proposal due date.

For a rep's own week, the opportunity list sorted by proposal due date is the more useful view. The calendar is for looking further out.
