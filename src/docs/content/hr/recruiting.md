Recruiting starts with a **requisition**, a formal request to hire with an approval step, so roles do not appear out of nowhere.

## Job requisitions

A requisition is the request. It carries:

- The role and division
- Why the hire is needed: growth, replacement, backfill
- Compensation range
- Who the role reports to
- Requested start date

### Raising one

1. Go to **HR → Recruiting → Job requisitions**.
2. Click **New requisition**.
3. Fill it in.
4. Submit for approval.

### Approval

Requisitions route for approval before anything is published. This is the control that keeps headcount deliberate. The conversation happens before a job ad exists, not after a candidate has been interviewed three times.

## The career page

Approved requisitions publish to your public career page. Applicants apply there, and they land in candidate tracking.

The career page is public and carries your branding. Read a posting as an outsider before publishing it. Internal shorthand that means nothing to a candidate is the most common problem.

## Candidate tracking

Every applicant, with their stage in your process.

Move candidates promptly. The most common recruiting failure is not rejecting people; it is leaving them in limbo for three weeks and losing the good ones.

::: tip
Reject early and clearly. A candidate rejected quickly and politely will apply again in two years or refer someone. One left hanging will not.
:::

## Resume parsing

Uploaded resumes are parsed to pull out contact details and experience, so you are reviewing rather than transcribing.

Check what it extracted before relying on it. Parsing is good, not perfect, and resume formats vary wildly.

## Interview scheduling

Schedule interviews and track who is meeting whom, when.

Worth deciding before you start rather than during: who is on the panel, what each person is assessing, and who makes the decision. Interview loops without that agreed up front produce five opinions and no decision.

## Candidate communication

Messages to candidates are tracked against their record, so anyone picking up the thread knows what has already been said.

Particularly useful when the person who started the conversation is on leave.

## From candidate to employee

When you decide to hire, the candidate moves to an **offer**. See [Offers and e-signatures](/docs/hr/offers-and-esignatures).

The candidate record carries across, so the offer knows who it is for and against which requisition.

## Keeping it clean

Two habits:

1. **Close requisitions when filled.** An open requisition that was filled in March still publishes to the career page.
2. **Archive old candidates.** A tracking list of four hundred people from three years ago is a list nobody opens.
