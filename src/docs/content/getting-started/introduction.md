ampOS is the system your company runs on. A job starts as a sales opportunity, becomes a scheduled job, gets test reports written against it in the field, turns into a deliverable the customer receives, and ends as an invoice. ampOS holds all of that in one place, and every step knows about the steps around it.

## What it replaces

Most electrical testing companies run on a pile of tools that do not talk to each other. ampOS covers the same ground in one app.

| Instead of | You use |
|---|---|
| Word templates for test reports | Built-in NETA report forms with test limits baked in |
| A shared drive of finished PDFs | Deliverables attached to the job that produced them |
| A spreadsheet of open jobs | The jobs list, filtered by status, division, or customer |
| A whiteboard of who is where | Scheduling and resource allocation |
| Guessing at margin | Live cost tracking and profitability per job |
| Re-typing invoices into QuickBooks | The QuickBooks integration |

## How the pieces fit together

Everything in ampOS hangs off a **job**. That is the single most important idea in the app.

::: note
If you only remember one thing: the job is the container. Reports, photos, costs, deliverables, change orders, and time all live on a job. When you cannot find something, open the job it belongs to.
:::

The normal flow looks like this:

1. **Sales** creates an opportunity and sends the customer an estimate.
2. When the customer says yes, that becomes a **job** with a job number.
3. The job gets **scheduled**: techs, dates, and a site.
4. In the field, techs create **reports** against the equipment they tested.
5. Reports get **reviewed and approved** by someone who did not write them.
6. Approved reports are bundled into a **deliverable** with a cover letter.
7. The deliverable goes to the customer, and the job is marked **ready to bill**.
8. Accounting bills it, and the numbers flow to **QuickBooks**.

You will not touch every step. A field tech lives in steps 4 and 5. An office admin lives in steps 6 through 8. That is fine. The docs are organized so you can read only your part.

## What makes it different from generic software

ampOS was built for electrical testing specifically, not adapted to it.

- **Real test reports.** Over 60 report forms that match ATS and MTS standards, with pass/fail limits, temperature correction, and print layouts that hold up when a customer's engineer reads them.
- **Divisions.** Field Tech, Lab, Calibration, Engineering, and regional divisions each get their own dashboard and their own view of the work.
- **Offline-tolerant field work.** Reports save as you type so a dropped connection in a substation does not cost you an afternoon.
- **Built-in review.** Nothing reaches a customer without someone approving it.
