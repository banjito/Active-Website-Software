Onboarding in ampOS is a set of **parallel checklists**: HR, IT, office admin, and the new hire's own forms. The tracking view shows all of them at once.

## Why parallel

A single sequential list means IT does not start until HR finishes. Parallel lists mean the laptop is ordered the day the offer is signed, not the day before the person arrives.

## New-hire packets

A packet bundles everything a new hire receives: forms to sign, policies to acknowledge, and information about the company.

Build packets per role type rather than per person. A field technician and an office admin need different things, and assembling it fresh each time is how something gets missed.

## The checklists

| Checklist | Owner | Typical items |
|---|---|---|
| **HR tasks** | HR | Employment forms, policy acknowledgments, benefits enrollment, payroll setup |
| **IT equipment** | IT | Laptop or tablet, phone, accounts, email, ampOS access |
| **Office admin** | Office | Badge, keys, parking, desk, business cards |
| **New hire's own** | The employee | Forms to sign, documents to read and acknowledge |

## The tracking view

**HR → Onboarding → Tracking** shows every in-flight onboarding and how far each checklist has got.

Check it weekly against upcoming start dates. A start date next Monday with an IT checklist untouched is the entire point of this view.

::: warning
The most common onboarding failure is the ampOS account. Someone completes the HR checklist perfectly and nobody creates the login, so a new technician spends their first morning watching. See [User management](/docs/admin/user-management).
:::

## E-sign forms

Forms the new hire signs electronically. They receive a link, sign, and the signed copy lands in their employee file automatically.

Forms can be **assigned to specific people** rather than sent to everyone, so a field technician signs a driving policy that an office admin does not.

## Welcome emails

Automated or templated messages sent before the start date.

Worth sending something in the gap between signature and first day. A candidate who signs and then hears nothing for three weeks starts wondering whether they made the right decision.

## The new hire's own view

The employee sees their own onboarding: what they need to do, what they have completed, what they are waiting on.

This alone removes most first-week confusion. People generally want to do the right thing and cannot tell what it is.

## Before day one

The list that actually matters:

1. ampOS account created, with role and division set
2. Equipment ordered and arriving
3. Badge and access arranged
4. Email working
5. Forms sent for signature
6. Someone assigned to meet them
7. First-week schedule agreed

Point 6 has no checklist item anywhere and matters more than most that do.

## After onboarding

Completed onboarding produces the [employee profile](/docs/hr/employee-profiles), with signed documents attached.

Review the checklists periodically. They accumulate steps that made sense once and now waste everyone's time.
