The sales portal covers everything that happens before a job exists: the customers you are talking to, the opportunities you are chasing, the estimates you have sent, and the goals you are measured against.

When an opportunity is **awarded**, it becomes a job, carrying its quoted amount across so profitability has something to measure against from day one.

## The sales dashboard

Open **Sales** from the portal. The dashboard answers four questions:

1. What is in the pipeline, and what is it worth?
2. What is closing this month?
3. What proposals are due?
4. Where does each rep stand against goal?

Click any number to jump to the filtered list behind it.

## The pieces

| Page | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Opportunities** | Deals you are chasing. The core of the portal. |
| **Estimates** | Priced proposals built from line items. |
| **Customers** | Companies, and their full history with you. |
| **Contacts** | The people at those companies. |
| **Territories** | Geographic or account-based slices, with reps assigned. |
| **Goals** | Targets, and progress against them. |
| **Pipeline calendar** | Opportunities plotted by expected close date. |
| **Estimating presets** | Saved rates so every quote starts from the same numbers. |
| **Mobile log** | Quick interaction logging from the field. |

## The flow

1. A conversation happens. Log it against the customer.
2. If it might become work, create an **opportunity**.
3. Build an **estimate** and generate the proposal.
4. Send it. Move the opportunity to `Quote`.
5. The customer decides. Move to `Decision`, then `Awarded` or `Lost`.
6. An awarded opportunity becomes a **job**.

## Logging interactions

Every call, visit, and email that matters should be logged against the customer. Use the **mobile log** from a phone in a parking lot; it takes fifteen seconds.

::: tip
The interaction log is what makes a customer record worth opening before a renewal conversation. A customer with six logged interactions tells you a story. One with none tells you nothing, and you will walk in cold.
:::

## Who sees what

Reps see their own opportunities and their territories. Sales management sees everything. Quoted amounts and margins are permission-gated, so field crews typically cannot see them.
