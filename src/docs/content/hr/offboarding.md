Offboarding runs the reverse of onboarding: a checklist of things to collect, revoke, and issue.

## Termination workflows

A workflow covers the whole departure, whichever kind it is: resignation, end of contract, or termination.

1. Go to **HR → Offboarding → Termination workflows**.
2. Start a workflow for the employee.
3. Set the last working day.
4. Work the checklist.

## The checklist

| Category | Items |
|---|---|
| **Access** | ampOS account, email, other systems |
| **Equipment** | Laptop, phone, test instruments, vehicle, tools |
| **Physical** | Badge, keys, fuel card, uniforms |
| **Records** | Final pay, benefits, references |
| **Handover** | Open jobs, opportunities, accounts, in-flight work |

## The one that gets missed

::: danger
**Deactivate the ampOS account.** It is easy to complete an HR offboarding checklist perfectly and leave the login active. Deactivate; do not delete. Deleting takes their report history with it, and every report they ever signed depends on that record existing.
:::

See [User management](/docs/admin/user-management).

## The other one that gets missed

Their [signature profile](/docs/deliverables/signature-profiles).

If it is not removed or updated, their name and phone number keep going out on customer documents. Months later, a customer calls the number on a report and reaches a disconnected line.

## Handover

Before the last day, reassign:

- Open jobs they were leading
- Opportunities and accounts they owned
- Anything they were the named contact for
- In-flight reports sitting in their queue

A report left in `In progress` by someone who has left blocks a deliverable, and it is not obvious why until someone goes looking.

## Exit surveys

Departing employees complete an exit survey through a link that does not require an account, so they can complete it after their access is gone.

Send it, and give them a few days after they leave. Answers given while still employed and answers given a week later are not the same answers.

### Reading them

One survey is an opinion. Six months of surveys is data.

Look for patterns rather than reacting to individual responses. Three people citing the same manager, or the same lack of progression, is a finding. One person having a bad experience may be too.

## Final documents

Issue whatever is required: final pay documentation, benefits continuation information, references, any certificates they hold.

Do this promptly. It is the last impression, and people talk to each other in a small industry.

## Rehire

Keep the record. Deactivated accounts and completed employee profiles mean a returning employee's history is intact rather than starting from zero.

Note in the record whether they are eligible for rehire. That question gets asked years later, by people who were not there.

## After the last day

1. Confirm access is actually revoked. Check, do not assume.
2. Confirm equipment was returned.
3. Confirm handover is complete and nothing is stuck in their name.
4. Send the exit survey.
5. Update the org chart.
